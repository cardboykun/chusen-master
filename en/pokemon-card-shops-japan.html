Where to Buy Pokémon Cards in Japan: 14 Card Shops a Local Collector Recommends (Akihabara, Yokohama & Sapporo)

Last checked: November 2026 · By じずしず (Jizushizu), the author of Chusen Master

Quick answers

  • Booster boxes at retail price? Very hard. New boxes in Japan are sold mostly by lottery (raffle), usually only to residents or members. In shops, sealed boxes are sold at secondary-market prices.
  • What you can buy: single cards, PSA-graded cards, sealed boxes at market price, original packs (oripa) and supplies. Retail-price packs show up now and then, usually with a limit per person.
  • Best area: Akihabara for the most shops on foot; Yokohama for three shops in one building by the station; Sapporo if you are visiting Hokkaido.
  • Bring: your passport (for tax-free shopping and for selling cards) and some cash in yen.

About me

I was born in Hokkaido and grew up in Sapporo, where I lived for 20 years before moving to Yokohama. I discovered Pokémon cards after I started working, fell in love with the cute artwork, and now collect cards and goods of my favorite Pokémon.

Based on my own experience, here are the card shops I recommend when you visit Japan — the ones I actually go to, plus a few big names that are worth knowing about. I also run 抽選マスターくん, a Japanese site that tracks Pokémon card lottery sales at Japanese retailers.

How buying Pokémon cards in Japan really works

Many visitors expect to walk into a shop and pick up booster boxes at the retail price. Unfortunately, that almost never happens. In Japan, new Pokémon booster boxes are mainly sold through lotteries: you apply in advance, and only the winners can buy. Most lotteries require a Japanese address, a membership or a store app, so they are hard to enter as a tourist.

What you can buy is sealed boxes at secondary-market prices. They cost more than retail, but they are available. Single booster packs at retail price are sometimes on sale, so if you come across them, you're in luck!

For most visitors, the real fun is in single cards, PSA-graded cards and supplies (sleeves, playmats, deck boxes). Japanese shops have huge stocks of singles, and you can often find Japanese-exclusive cards and promos.

Tips and common shop rules

  • Purchase limits: on new releases, shops often set limits such as "10 packs per person per day" or "one box per person". Limits are posted in the shop or on its X account on the day.
  • Check X before you go: almost every shop posts stock, new arrivals, hours changes and sale information on X (Twitter). The links are listed for each shop below.
  • Handle cards with care: ask staff before taking cards out of display cases, and put storage cards back where you found them.
  • Play spaces are mainly for tournaments and customers; check the rules posted in each shop.
  • Selling cards (buyback) needs photo ID by Japanese law. Bring your passport — some shops only buy from Japanese residents.
  • Many shops are on upper floors of buildings. Look for signs at street level and take the elevator.

Tax-free shopping (new rules from November 2026)

Only shops registered as tax-free stores can sell to you tax-free. Since November 1, 2026, Japan uses a refund system: you pay the price including the 10% consumption tax at the shop, and the tax is refunded when you leave Japan, after customs at the airport or seaport checks the goods. Bring your passport when shopping, keep the items with you until departure, and leave extra time at the airport. Most card shops below do not announce tax-free service, so ask at the counter before you pay.

Other places to look

Besides specialist card shops, big stores such as AEON, Yodobashi Camera, Bic Camera and Ito-Yokado sell card supplies, and sometimes cards too — worth a quick look if you pass by. Their toy floors also have plenty of Japanese games and hobby items, from Nintendo Switch and PlayStation 5 games to Gundam model kits, so you're sure to have fun.

FAQ

Can I buy Pokémon card booster boxes at retail price in Japan?

Rarely. New booster boxes are sold mainly through lotteries (raffles) run by shops and retailers, and most of them need a Japanese address or a membership. In card shops you can buy sealed boxes, but at secondary-market prices, which are usually higher than the retail price.

What can tourists actually buy at Japanese card shops?

Single cards, PSA and other graded cards, sealed boxes at market price, original packs ("oripa"), sleeves, playmats and other supplies. Single booster packs at retail price appear only occasionally and usually with a per-person limit.

Which area is best for a card shop crawl?

Akihabara in Tokyo has the most shops within walking distance. In Yokohama, three shops share one building next to the station. In Sapporo, the shops are clustered around Odori.

Can I shop tax-free at card shops in Japan?

Only at shops registered as tax-free stores. From November 1, 2026, Japan uses a refund system: you pay the price including tax at the shop, then get the consumption tax back at the airport or seaport when you leave Japan, after customs checks the goods. Bring your passport and ask the shop if it is tax-free before buying.

Do card shops in Japan take credit cards?

Many large chains accept credit cards, e-money and QR code payments, but not every shop says so. Carry enough cash (Japanese yen) just in case.
